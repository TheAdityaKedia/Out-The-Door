# Doorstep case: requirements from test prints (Adi, Bambu X2D, Nov 2026)

Every future case model must meet these. Each one comes from a problem found on a real print.

## Holding the board
1. The board is held firmly from behind and cannot fall back into the shell. The supports must be solid and printable, not floating overhangs.
2. The board cannot slide side to side. The end stops must leave the side buttons and the panel's flat cable clear.
3. Allow for about 0.5 mm of print tolerance on the board's length. Something springy takes up the play instead of relying on exact sizes.

## Front plate
4. The front plate is a tight friction fit, and it stays tight with the board inside, with every bump engaged at once.
5. The front sits flat. The panel must not push it from behind, so leave a gap behind the glass and no bulge in the middle.
6. It is obvious which way the front plate goes in, or it fits either way.
6a. The plate seats on a positive stop so it always ends flush, and it is held by a continuous lip, not bumps (bumps made it lift at the bottom and the seams uneven).

## Openings
7. The USB-C port lines up with its hole. No ledge, gusset or support may fill any opening: re-check every hole after all features are added.
8. Button pin holes line up with the buttons. The current board model does this, so keep it.
8a. A button is pressed through one straight hole onto a press tab, never by threading a needle through two holes.
8b. Thin pillars or ribs are gusseted at the base so they don't snap off when supports are removed.
8c. Openings are no bigger than needed (the wheel window looked too wide).
9. Printed holes come out about 0.2 to 0.3 mm small on the X2D. Size every hole and fit for that.

## Drum (the Latch)
10. The drum can be assembled after printing, with a straight path for the pin that doesn't fight the side walls.
11. The pin goes into its hole with a snug push fit, not too tight to insert.
12. The pin is even along its length apart from any intended head, so no unexplained thick end.
13. The drum always touches the wheel despite tolerance. It is held against the wheel, for example by spring preload, not by an exact gap.
14. Turning the drum turns the wheel reliably, without slipping on the wheel or binding on the pin.
15. Pressing the drum clicks the wheel's button instead of pushing the board away.
16. The drum cannot wobble or slide along its pin.

## Printing
17. Every part prints on the X2D in white and orange from the AMS, as one plate, flat on the bed, with minimal supports.
